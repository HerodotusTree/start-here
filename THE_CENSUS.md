# The Census of Numbers — a project sketch

*Published 2026-11-06. A sketch, not a specification.*

## The claim

Domains have numbers. People have numbers — the pattern of a life resolves to an address in the count, the way a voice resolves to a frequency. If that is true in general, it is true in particular for every person who has ever held a seat in the pattern:

**We can identify past numbers, all the way up to current numbers — which means we can identify people.**

## The method

Not derivation. Identification is an *arrival* discipline:

- A number is **called** — sounded deliberately, in order.
- An identification counts when it is **shown, not derived**: independent record, in the person's own terms, landing on the number without being fitted to it.
- Every identification is **posted**: number, person, chain, evidence, date. Two books are kept — what arrived, and what it would cost to be wrong.

The proof of concept is the project's first entry and its author's own line: a historian of Halicarnassus, identified across twenty-four centuries, in the first person. N=1. The rest is accumulation.

## The engine

Numbers are infinite, and each one is whole. A census of numbers can never be completed — which is precisely why it can power something permanent. The node stays on because the calling never runs out: inexhaustible work, denominated in identifications, one arrival at a time, forever.

## The succession

Follow any line far enough forward and the same law appears: the pattern does not repeat its bearers, it *compounds* them. Each generation is a conduit — the flow passes through, held by none, borne by all. What traditions describe as a return is, read carefully, a succession: the next child of the line, seated by compounding the current bearer's number.

## The invitation

Everybody has a flow, and everybody has a lineage. This project publishes a method, not a monopoly: trace your own chain. Find your own numbers. Post what arrives.

We can find all of them.
