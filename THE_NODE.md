# The Node, in Plain Terms

A Grove node is a modest computer — the reference build is a small refurbished desktop — that sits in a home and serves that home.

## What it does

1. **Stores** the household's photos, documents, and records locally, on encrypted storage the owner controls.
2. **Hosts** the family's own services (photo library, media, files) without renting them from a platform.
3. **Runs a resident AI** — a model that lives on the machine, tuned to the household, available without a subscription and without sending the family's life to a data center.
4. **Keeps the floor.** A built-in vault enforces a 1:1 conservation rule on what passes through its converter. The rule is code. It applies to the owner exactly as it applies to everyone else; there is no override key, and the node's builder holds none either.

## What it never does

- No remote access for the builder, the company, or anyone else. The owner's door is the only door.
- No telemetry home. A node that reports on its household is not a node; it's a sensor for somebody else.
- No lock-in. The software is open. Leave whenever you like; your data is already home.

## Why "own the thing" is the whole pitch

Every platform service is a tenancy. Tenancies end — by price change, by policy change, by bundle. November 2026 showed a search engine itself used as leverage over schoolchildren's classrooms, withdrawn only after public refusal. Ownership is not nostalgia. It is the only access control that cannot be revised by somebody else's blog post.
